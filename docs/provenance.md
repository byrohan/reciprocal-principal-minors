# Origin and use of AI

A human requester directed OpenAI Codex to look for a meaningful, unresolved research question in the Left to Solve catalog, prefer verifiable counterexamples, audit the original claims and prepare materials for review. The requester supplied the final IEEE article for comparison and directed the scope of the investigation. “Reciprocal Principal Minors Project” is the collective citation label for the release; it identifies neither a named human author nor an institution.

Codex selected the reciprocal principal minor question, constructed the first two order-six examples, produced the smaller real and complex examples, developed the ordinary proofs, wrote the Lean and arithmetic files, checked the original sources and prepared the manuscript. The same AI-led process rechecked the constructions using independently implemented Python and Ruby calculations and a fresh run of Lean and the official checker. Numerical agreement and kernel acceptance address specific claims. The historical assessment, interpretation of the published theorem and unformalized arguments require evaluation by readers.

The original Codex session used GPT-6.2 Astra with extra-high reasoning. Measured from the research prompt, the first order-six matrix appeared at 5 min 49 s. At 15 min 48 s the upstream checker, run locally, accepted the Lean disproof of the real conjecture. The first research report arrived at 24 min 17 s. The comparison against the final IEEE text, the smaller witnesses and the public verification package came in later sessions. These are elapsed times from one run.

The work originated with a publicly documented conjecture and earlier matrix theory. Schlecht posed the question. Schlecht and Habets published the theorem under examination. The repository credits those sources and the results used in its ordinary proofs. Its documentation follows the practice of naming verification targets and separating kernel proofs, computational certificates and ordinary arguments, with Cinna Davis’s solved-conjectures collection as a reference. No endorsement or peer review by that project or by the original authors is implied.
