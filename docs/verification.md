# Verification report

**Conclusion.** The real conjecture is false. An order-five real matrix also satisfies every hypothesis and violates both alternatives of the complex Theorem 7 in the published IEEE article. The smallest complex example uses order four. The evidence for the individual claims has different scopes.

| Claim | What verifies it |
|---|---|
| Negation of the universally quantified real conjecture | **Full Lean theorem.** `OMA.disproof` constructs the original order-six integer matrix over the reals, proves invertibility, irreducibility, equality on every labeled subset and failure of both diagonal alternatives. The local official checker independently accepts this disproof. [Real statement](../ReciprocalMinors/Statement.lean) · [Proof](../ReciprocalMinors/Six.lean) |
| An order-five matrix violates the published complex Theorem 7 | **Full Lean witness theorem.** `ReciprocalFive.certificate` proves the instantiated hypotheses over the complex field and excludes every allowable complex diagonal in both alternatives. Its entries happen to be real. Matching the journal’s printed mathematical notation to the Lean types is a human source check. [Formal witness](../ReciprocalMinors/Five.lean) |
| Order four suffices over the complex field | **Lean checks a numerator certificate.** It proves an inverse-product identity after clearing denominators, labeled minor equalities, a nonzero Hamilton cycle and two support/product obstructions. The paper interprets the numerator identities over the complex field. An exact second implementation checks the complex rational matrices. [Gaussian certificate](../ReciprocalMinors/Four.lean) · [Exact computation](../scripts/exact_complex.py) |
| Dense examples with positive principal minors | **Lean checks the dense order-six numerator certificate.** Scaling by 107 and cycle-product invariance give the matrix claim. Exact arithmetic also checks both dense real examples, all entries and all labeled minors. [Dense certificate](../ReciprocalMinors/Six.lean) · [Exact table](../verification/expected/all-minors.csv) |
| Least possible failing orders five (real) and four (complex); infinite families; rank-cut repair | **Ordinary mathematical proofs** in §§2–5, with cited matrix results. The finite four-dimensional enumeration is an additional regression calculation, confined to the specified ternary entries. [Manuscript](../paper/manuscript.pdf) |
| Published Appendix B equations and deletion error | **Ordinary deductions and exact arithmetic.** The half-parameter pair satisfies the displayed normal forms, fails (71) and (72), and gives a nonzero value for (73). The scripts separately check the correct mixed-minor transformation. [Equation verifier](../scripts/journal_equations.rb) · [Results](../verification/expected/journal-equations.json) |
| Alternative criterion for networks lossless for all delay tuples | **Ordinary mathematical proof** in §7 using stable determinantal representation theory. Its application and historical priority have no formal certificate. [Manuscript](../paper/manuscript.pdf) · [Al Ahmadieh and Vinzant](https://doi.org/10.1093/imrn/rnae038) |

## Arithmetic checks

The real verifier compares the same labeled subset on both sides, including the empty set. Across the two original order-six examples it checks 64 + 64 equalities. The order-five integer and dense examples add 32 + 32, and the order-four complex example adds 16. The total is **208**. The normalized pair used to test Appendix B is checked separately. Invertibility is checked by products, irreducibility by graph reachability, and failure of diagonal similarity by inconsistent entry ratios, support or cycle products. Exact integers, rational numbers and Gaussian arithmetic avoid floating-point tolerances. [Expected records](../verification/expected)
