# Sources, prior work and historical priority

The counterexamples disprove the stated implication over its original field and under its stated hypotheses. The historical review completed on 26 November 2026 located no earlier published disproof of that precise implication. A priority claim would require stronger evidence. The archival record includes an affirmative proof claim in 2016, followed by a peer-reviewed publication and a dissertation. It would be inaccurate to describe the question as universally recognized as open throughout that period.

## The printed assertion and its implications

The published Theorem 7 on p. 1558 assumes that $A$ is invertible and irreducible, and that its entrywise conjugate and inverse have the same corresponding principal minors. It concludes diagonal similarity of the inverse to either the conjugate or the conjugate transpose. The authors expressly drop the earlier cut-rank condition on p. 1562. Universal losslessness enters the statement of Theorem 8 on p. 1559.

The induction in Appendix B on p. 1562 can produce principal deletions that are no longer inverse pairs. Its order-four argument on p. 1563 uses invalid identities for mixed minors in normalized forms (69)–(70). Using the journal’s one-based indexing, the two sides of (71) are $(16-20i)/41$ and $(162-18i)/41$. The claimed zero in (73) evaluates to $(-1172-52i)/41$. The published numbers and mathematical content agree with the preprint. [Exact calculation](../scripts/journal_equations.rb) · [Values for (71)–(73)](../verification/expected/journal-equations.json)

The proof of Theorem 8 uses Theorem 7. The necessity argument in Theorem 1 then invokes Theorem 8. The literal sufficient condition in Theorem 1 permits an indefinite nonsingular diagonal form. The abstract’s intended characterization by diagonal similarity to unitary blocks has a separate ordinary proof in manuscript §7. The journal adds §IV-D on practical design. Its three operations — unitary mixing, diagonal rescaling and block triangular connection — each have an independent sufficient-condition argument. The findings provide no evidence of a failed deployed reverberator. [Manuscript, §§6–7](../paper/manuscript.pdf)

## Search coverage and limitations

The investigation checked the article DOI and title with terms for errata, corrections, comments, retractions, counterexamples and alternative proofs. Matrix-theory queries included complementary and reciprocal principal minors, principal minor assignment and equivalence, property D, rank-one cuts, cut transpose, clan reversal, Cayley transforms and determinantal representations. General equivalence results explain how equal principal minors can coexist with distinct diagonal-similarity classes. That leaves the extra condition that the designated partner be the inverse or conjugate inverse.
